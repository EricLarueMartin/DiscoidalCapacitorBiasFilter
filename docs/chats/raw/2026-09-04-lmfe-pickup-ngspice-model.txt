Reconstructed collaboration record, 2026-09-04

User requested a maintained ngspice version of the LEGEND-200 LMFE detector
front end, based on the local LTspice source model, with op-amp bias filters
removed, retained op amps idealized, and the downstream differential-output
section removed. The model is to use the Designer detector-capacitance value
for C19, defaulting to a conservative 10 pF, and add a response line to the
complete-filter SPICE slide. A new model slide belongs in Models & FEA results.

The first simplified LTspice schematic produced a singular-matrix message.
Inspection showed that +VB1 had been dropped. The user restored it and
confirmed that the simplified frequency response changes slightly but retains
the structure needed for noise response and detector input impedance.

Implemented a reusable ngspice subcircuit and standalone deck under
simulations/spice. The reduced model retains MX11RC, BF862, detector C19,
feedback, signal-line delays, and the differential voltage across R30. It uses
finite-gain, finite-bandwidth ideal op amps in place of LMH6654 vendor models.
The backend connects the detector cable to the complete filter output, injects
a 1 A AC current at the detector cathode, and records both pickup
transimpedance and detector-node impedance. The slide trace is normalized to
the maximum transimpedance in the sweep because raw V/A cannot honestly share
a V/V attenuation axis.

The first integrated ngspice-44.2 check on the development Pi completed with
161 frequency points from 1 Hz through 100 MHz. It produced approximately
2.38 kohm peak pickup transimpedance, 36.8 Mohm detector-node impedance at
1 Hz, and 37 ohm detector-node impedance at 100 MHz for the current default
design. These values remain simulation results pending measurement.

Follow-up request: determine whether raising the filter output capacitance
above its roughly 40 pF reference value materially reduces pickup induced on
the cryostat HV cable. Use the Designer output series resistor, default it to
the stocked 47 ohm value, apply the configured trapezoidal filter, and
normalize pickup response by an otherwise identical SPICE run with signal
injected at the MX11RC J2 gate. Sweep the output-stage capacitance from 0.1 to
100 times its FEA value and explain the two excitations on separate slides.

Implemented a seven-case sweep at factors 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, and 100. The
reference final-node FEA capacitance is 44.826 pF. For each case ngspice solves
a cathode-current pickup excitation and a one-volt J2-gate signal excitation.
The pickup is input-referred as Zpickup/(Z0 Ggate), followed by the configured
trapezoidal response. With the provisional 0.05 mV rms white plus 0.20 mV rms
50 Hz cryostat pickup, the reference case gives 0.339 eV rms detector-equivalent
noise. The 0.1x and 100x endpoints give 0.352 and 0.0653 eV rms respectively.
The simulated gain from adding capacitance is therefore negligible relative to
the approximately 831 eV rms Ge Fano scale and does not presently justify the
additional partial-discharge risk of discrete high-voltage capacitors.
