Full-wave model scope

The displayed full-field sweep starts at 50 Hz using the FEniCSx electroquasistatic Maxwell limit. MEEP takes over at the shared 1 GHz overlap point. Optional resonance verification uses a coarse sweep followed by fine logarithmic brackets around measured peaks and low-loss RLC predictions.

The structure can be electrically small and still couple through fringing electric fields, common-mode currents, leads, and apertures. The grounded tube is therefore part of the electromagnetic model, not merely a cavity-resonance precaution.

Each core section receives its own effective conductivity, σ = L/(RA). The input section uses the entered input resistance, every interior section uses the stage resistance, and the output section uses the entered output resistance.

The MEEP branch includes only rotationally symmetric m = 0 fields. Leads, a helical MELF film, and asymmetric antenna or cavity modes require a later 3D solve.

What the repeating-cell approximation solves

FEniCSx meshes and solves only two unique axial regions:

  1. End region: the physical end cap through the axial center of the first bias plate.
  2. Interior half-cell: the center of that first bias plate through the center of the next interior ground plate—the second ground plate when the end cap is counted.

The far end is an exact mirror of the solved end region. Between the two ends, the interior half-cell is copied and alternately mirrored about successive bias-plate and ground-plate center planes until the reconstructed stack reaches the far-end region.

The two-region FEA is valid for the equal-voltage all-bias field and total-capacitance screen. Its fast SPICE network allocates regional energy to end/interior nodes and uses an analytical adjacent Cpar; those entries are not Maxwell row sums or differential FEA extractions.

This reduction is never applied to asymmetric single-node basis fields. When unchecked, those capacitance fields use the full stack; only an exactly reflected partner excitation may be reused. The validation CLI can also disable reflection averaging for an independent full-model reference.

Calculation method

The temperature control evaluates thermally induced stress at a selected assembly temperature; it is not a design target.

This is a closed-form thermal-mismatch estimate, not a thermal-stress FEA. Below the 9510 glass transition at 70 °C, the rigid cured epoxy wants to contract much more than alumina or copper. The estimate uses σ = kEΔαΔT/(1 − ν) and compares it with the published 20 MPa cured tensile strength.

k is the fraction of radial and circumferential (hoop) mismatch strain prevented in the closed-form approximation. It assumes σrr = σθθ and σzz = σrz = 0. It is not a prescribed surface shift, and it does not control the FEA.

Axisymmetry removes variation with circumferential angle, but radial displacement still changes circumference, so hoop strain and stress remain. The FEA includes σrr, σzz, σθθ, and σrz. Local three-direction confinement can therefore exceed the radial-plus-hoop plane-stress baseline. At k = 0.5, the 20 MPa bulk-strength proxy crossing moves to --.

The FEniCSx model solves the selected plate and tube conductors, core, epoxy, and any distinct solid washer with axisymmetric linear elasticity and exact center-plane symmetry. Selecting an epoxy-filled gap removes the washer material interface. All remaining interfaces are perfectly bonded, so the model reports epoxy stress but cannot predict separation. A separation-temperature solve requires cohesive interface laws and adhesion or fracture data for the actual material pairs and surface preparations.

The published 3.9% cure shrinkage is not added as elastic strain because the locked-in fraction after gel is unknown. Any retained cure stress reduces the available cold margin.

Detector bias filtering

Discoidal Capacitor Bias Filter

A compact, axisymmetric high-voltage, low-current filter with a resistive core.

Simulation geometry: built-in defaults.

What Is Modeled

  • Electrostatics: axisymmetric FEniCSx fields, dielectric stress, and a node-resolved Maxwell capacitance matrix.
  • Circuit response: the complete unbuffered resistance-capacitance ladder, cable, detector, and DC load in SPICE.
  • Mechanical screening: a separate axisymmetric thermoelastic model for bonded epoxy, ceramic, copper, and core solids.
  • Symmetry and repeated cells are used only for excitations and regions that actually preserve them.

Electrical Model for SPICE Simulation

  • Rstage connects bias nodes and remains entered directly or calculated from resistive-core geometry.
  • Exact mode extracts each Cg,i and Cpar,i,j from full-stack Maxwell basis fields.
  • Fast repeated-cell mode uses a regional Cg allocation and analytical adjacent Cpar for screening.
  • End resistances occur once. Cable, detector, and load conductance appear only at the final node.
  • SPICE uses the selected exact or screening network without treating the two as equivalent evidence.

SPICE Simulation of the Complete Filter

  • The backend solves every R, Cg, and Cpar element in the unbuffered ladder.
  • Exact mode uses the extracted Maxwell network; repeated mode uses the explicitly approximate screening network.
  • The loaded curve includes the cable transmission line, detector capacitance, and DC load conductance once at the final node.
  • The unloaded curve removes the cable and detector load to show voltage-noise attenuation at the bare filter output.
  • The 50 Ω test curve adds a tracking-generator source and analyzer load, including source mismatch without changing the filter input resistance.
  • The intrinsic curve also removes the source-side network, matching the direct-drive, open-output ports used by the field solve.
  • End resistors occur only where specified; the complete solve avoids multiplying an isolated one-section response.

Noise-based stopping point

Bias Noise After the Energy Filter

  • A 2039 keV event creates about 689,000 electron-hole pairs, or 110 fC. The three-measurement weighted default F = 0.1145 gives about 0.831 keV rms of irreducible Fano fluctuation.
  • Bias noise is propagated through the complete loaded ladder and the selected discrete trapezoidal energy-filter response.
  • The public LEGEND reference estimator uses a 10 µs rise and 4 µs flat top; current production settings remain an input to confirm.
  • The design criterion is predicted bias-path noise below 0.10 times the calculated Fano RMS fluctuation; observed detector resolution is deliberately excluded.
  • The provisional source envelope remains deliberately adjustable because a peak-to-peak supply limit and an assumed laboratory pickup level do not define measured spectra.

Geometry to expected performance

From Full-Geometry FEA to SPICE

  • Electrostatic FEniCSx basis fields extract each bias node's Cg and every mutual Cpar directly from the complete geometry.
  • A distributed electroquasistatic field solve agrees with the matched RC network within 0.62 dB through the saved 1 Hz–100 MHz sweep.
  • The axisymmetric full-wave result has no resonance-like feature through 1 GHz. An earlier exploratory sweep found a candidate wavelength-scale mode near 10 GHz, not RC agreement through 10 GHz.
  • The extracted capacitances and specified or geometry-derived resistances are used for the complete loaded SPICE ladder.

Bias-to-Ground Capacitance from FEA

  • A one-bias geometry gives Cg directly because no bias-to-bias capacitance exists.
  • In the three-bias geometry, the middle-only result Cm includes capacitance to both grounded neighboring bias plates and therefore overcounts Cg.
  • The neighbors-only and all-bias states remove that mutual contribution: Cg,m = (Cm − Cn + Ca)/2.
  • Exact full-design values use the Maxwell row sum Cg,i = ΣjCij; repeated screening values do not claim this extraction.
  • The resistive core remains dielectric during capacitance extraction and is not treated as an electrode.

Bias-to-Bias Parasitic Capacitance from FEA

  • The panels show the actual full-stack r-z region and representative boundary potentials returned by FEniCSx.
  • The brown core is dielectric with its selected effective permittivity; only the solid bias-plate cross sections are electrodes.
  • Exact reflected basis fields are reused. For two bias plates, node B is the mirror of node A and is not solved separately.
  • The mirrored field is reused directly in the Maxwell cross-energy integral; the all-bias energy remains an independent total check.
  • For three plates, only one end, the center, and all-bias states are solved; the far-end basis is reused by reflection.
  • The two-node panel is reconstructed from the linear basis fields as a visual check, not an additional solve.
  • Cpar,i,j is the negative off-diagonal Maxwell coefficient, −Cij.

Peak Electric Field from FEA

  • The peak field must converge as the mesh is refined before it is used as a breakdown limit.

Default geometry · matched open-circuit ports

Electroquasistatic FEA Checks the Distributed RC Model

  • The input bias plate is fixed at 1 V, all interior plates float, and the output plate is measured open-circuit.
  • No input resistor, output resistor, cable, detector capacitance, or load conductance appears in either curve.
  • The complete complex admittance matrix comes from the distributed electric field and continuous resistive-core surrogate—not from inserted capacitances.
  • At 1 kHz, FEniCSx gives -10.08 dB and intrinsic SPICE gives -10.03 dB; their worst saved difference is 0.62 dB.
  • Reciprocity is exact numerically; the real admittance is passive; 1 kHz changes by only 0.00014 dB from 0.60 to 0.30 mm mesh.
  • This validates the electroquasistatic RC limit; it does not include magnetic induction, cavity modes, or radiation.

Low-frequency field limit through first mode

Full-Field Noise Transmission to First Resonance

  • The displayed full-field sweep starts at 50 Hz with the electroquasistatic Maxwell solution, which is the correct low-frequency limit for this millimetre-scale geometry.
  • At the shared 1 GHz overlap point it hands off to cylindrical m = 0 MEEP and continues logarithmically until the first local resonance is bracketed.
  • MEEP is normalized to the EQS transfer at 1 GHz because the present radial source is not yet an impedance-calibrated wave port.
  • Noise-power attenuation is −10 log10(Sout/Sin) = −20 log10|Vout/Vin| for the same voltage-noise reference.
  • The default design's first candidate mode is near 10 GHz. Leads, cable common mode, and non-axisymmetric modes still require 3D validation.

Frequency-domain model ladder

From Damped Resonances to Field-Derived Transfer

  • The frequency-domain network combines the FEniCSx Maxwell capacitance matrix with geometry-derived partial inductance for each axial current path.
  • A low-loss reference exposes the coupled LC modes; the physical interstage resistance strongly damps those peaks.
  • A single-mode RLC check chooses Leff so its zero-resistance natural frequency matches the first coupled-model peak, then applies the same physical resistance.
  • The matched oscillator and coupled network should agree on resonance scale and whether the physical design is underdamped or overdamped; their full transfer amplitudes need not match.
  • The intrinsic comparison applies 1 V directly to the input plate, leaves interior plates floating, measures the output open-circuit, and calculates Vout/Vin from a field-derived complex admittance matrix.
  • EQS includes distributed conduction and displacement current, but not magnetic induction, skin effect, radiation, or the 3D MELF helix.
  • After EQS-to-SPICE validation, the next check is a 3D curl-curl solution followed by a measured S-parameter or impedance sweep.

Current thinking

Way Forward

Design direction

  • Keep the symmetric washer/core stack as the baseline.
  • Prefer ferrite only if measured resistance and excess noise justify it.
  • Keep matched discrete HV capacitors as a symmetric fallback, not the primary design.

Close the evidence gaps

  1. Converge electrostatic peak field and capacitance.
  2. Measure core resistance, noise, leakage, and partial discharge.
  3. Run and validate the 3D frequency-domain electromagnetic model.
  4. Compare the complete prototype with the SPICE and EM predictions.

Possible mechanical limitation

Possible Thermal Limits of Heat-Cured Epoxy

  • The closed form assumes equal radial and hoop stress with zero axial and r-z shear stress.
  • The FEA maximum-principal result combines radial, axial, hoop, and r-z shear stress and is the most limiting trend shown.
  • The 20 MPa horizontal line is retained as a common bulk-strength comparison proxy from the 9510 data; it is not a Stycast tensile value or a predicted interface-separation temperature.
  • FEA reports the 99th-percentile epoxy maximum-principal tensile stress; its raw material-corner peak remains mesh sensitive.
  • 9510 has no published bulk shear strength. Its substrate-specific lap-shear values cannot be used as the local FEA shear limit; the actual copper/alumina bonds need coupon data.
  • The dashed k = 0.5 FEA trend is a linear stress-scaling sensitivity, not a second interface-compliance solve.

Candidate limitation

Available MELF Resistance Falls Short at 50/60 Hz

  • The available 12 MΩ MELF value is much lower than the desired per-stage resistance.
  • Alumina is practical and robust, but its modest permittivity limits the shunt capacitance.
  • Its intrinsic section attenuation is about 0.1 dB at 50 Hz; the full loaded response still falls short of the desired suppression.
  • The result motivates a higher-resistance ferrite path or a more complex MELF topology.

Backup: optional symmetric shunt

Discrete HV Capacitors Stay a Backup

Model the fallback

  • If extra Cg becomes necessary, use at least two matched HV capacitors placed symmetrically—never one asymmetric part.
  • Additional Cg applies the same ideal shunt at every bias node. Zero is the baseline; one node is sensitivity-only.

Why it is disfavored

  • Previous dry-encapsulated HV-capacitor assemblies suffered discharges; liquid potting is unavailable.
  • Only pursue this branch if the washer/core design cannot meet attenuation without it.

Qualification gate: test the symmetric assembly for partial discharge, leakage, loss, noise, and thermal cycling. The ideal input omits package voids, placement Cpar, and lead inductance.

Thin Epoxy Reduces Volume, Not Necessarily Local Stress

Backup: prototype test sequence

Measure the Ferrite Before Trusting the Model

  1. Characterize the ferrite. Cut nominal 2.0 mm samples and measure resistance versus length, voltage, temperature, and time.
  2. Measure noise scaling. Compare absolute and normalized resistance-noise spectra with MELF, and compare another readily available diameter if useful.
  3. Qualify the washer. Inspect the purchased alumina for dimensions, flatness, chips, finish, and trapped-interface risk.
  4. Dry-fit both cores. Confirm that cut 2.0 mm ferrite and 2.2 mm MELF inserts use identical plates, washers, alignment, and clearances.
  5. Use the epoxy sample. Make small wetting and thermal-cycle coupons before potting the full stack.
  6. Compare complete filters. Measure attenuation, leakage, discharge pulses, drift, and low-frequency noise under the same bias and load.

Possible later hybrid: ferrite stages first for 50/60 Hz attenuation, followed by MELF stages near the detector if the ferrite has measurable excess noise.

Backup: circuit model

One Section Matches; Stage Multiplication Does Not

  • For one Rstage–Cg section with no additional end resistance or external load, the analytical divider and full MNA/SPICE solve are identical.
  • With multiple unbuffered sections, downstream capacitors load the upstream nodes, so their transfer functions cannot be multiplied as independent stages.
  • The section-count approximation also repeats the cable and detector load at every section instead of applying it once at the output.
  • The full ladder solve, not the multiplied estimate, is used for design conclusions.

Backup: Cpar solver consistency

Full, Mirror, and Repeated Models Agree

  • The reference meshes the complete stack and applies no symmetry to the extracted matrix.
  • The mirror case averages only exactly reflected Maxwell-matrix entries.
  • The repeated case additionally shares fully interior entries; end coupling remains distinct.
  • For two through five bias plates, all three methods give the same average adjacent Cpar.
  • The default geometry gives FEA/analytic ≈ 0.478 because the intervening ground plate shields the axial path.

Backup: Cpar analytical limit

Cpar Converges Toward the Axial Approximation

  • Use three bias plates in a uniform εr = 3.4 dielectric.
  • Make the ground plate a narrow outer annulus and move it and the grounded tube radially outward.
  • Increase the bias-disc diameter with the ground-hole diameter so most field energy is axial.
  • FEA/εA/d rises monotonically from 0.720 at 24 mm OD to 0.949 at 120 mm OD.
  • This limiting geometry verifies that ordinary designs fall below the parallel-plate estimate because of shielding, not a capacitance scaling error.

Backup: Cg end effects

Cg Matrix: Two vs Three Bias Plates

  • The two-bias matrix has one unique end-node basis field; the opposite end is its exact reflection.
  • The three-bias matrix has two unique basis fields: one end and the center. The far end reuses the first field by reflection.
  • Comparing the two-bias value with the three-bias end and center values exposes finite-stack end effects.
  • The reflected field supplies the remaining cross-energy coefficients; the all-bias energy independently verifies the row-sum total.
  • For two or three plates, Repeat interior adds no further class: there is either no interior node or only one.

Backup: Cg solver consistency

Cg Matrix Treatments Agree

  • The campaign includes the previously omitted one-bias case, then two through five bias plates.
  • The reference uses the complete stack with no matrix symmetry; exact mirror averages only reflected entries.
  • Repeating treatment additionally shares interior-node classes while preserving total Cg.

Backup: Cg analytical limit

Cg Converges Toward Two Flat Washers

  • Use one bias plate between two ground plates so center-plane mirror symmetry is exact.
  • Use a 2.0 mm core, 2.2 mm ground ID, and uniform εr = 3.4 dielectric.
  • Increase matched washer and bias OD from 12 to 120 mm while retaining a 2 mm radial tube gap.
  • FEA/(2εA/d) falls monotonically from 1.449 to 1.038.
  • The positive excess is fringing capacitance; its fractional contribution shrinks as plate area grows.

  • Initial three-bias wide-disc batches were stopped near 5.7 GiB RSS before swap or OOM.

Backup: limiting geometry

Radius and Gap Set a Conservative Field Bound

  • Let r be the minimum radius on any exposed conductor feature.
  • Let d be the minimum dielectric gap to the opposite conductor.
  • The limiting reference is a radius-r sphere inside a radius-(r + d) grounded sphere.
  • Use Emax = V(r + d)/(r d), then audit the actual geometry with FEA.

Backup: geometry optimization

Field-Limited Packing Has a Simple Shape Optimum

  • Assume plate thickness is 2r, dielectric gap is d, and stack pitch is L = d + 2r.
  • Combine gap capacitance with the radius-gap field limit.
  • The resulting capacitance-per-length-per-field metric is optimized at r/d = 1/√2.
