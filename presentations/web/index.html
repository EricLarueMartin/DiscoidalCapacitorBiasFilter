Calculation sequence

The electrical FEA supplies every bias-node capacitance to ground and the mutual-capacitance matrix. SPICE combines that network with the selected input, stage, and output resistances, source cable, regulator impedance, output cable, and detector load.

With Repeat interior FEA cell enabled, a solved stack of at least three bias plates supplies end/interior Cg and Cpar classes for any plate count of three or more. Changing plate count or resistor values then needs only SPICE. Three bias plates have two interplate resistors; five have four, in addition to the configured input/output resistors. Changing local dimensions, permittivity, or disabling repeat mode requires matching FEA. Exact full-stack and RF results still depend on plate count.

Changing settings never starts a simulation. Finish your edits, then press Run SPICE simulation. Slides list any missing simulations in place of their graphics.

Full-wave model scope

The displayed full-field sweep starts at 50 Hz using the FEniCSx electroquasistatic Maxwell limit. EQS and MEEP share 300 MHz and 1 GHz: the lower point anchors provisional port normalization and the upper point tests agreement before handoff. Optional resonance verification uses a coarse sweep followed by fine logarithmic brackets around measured peaks and low-loss RLC predictions.

The structure can be electrically small and still couple through fringing electric fields, common-mode currents, leads, and apertures. The grounded tube is therefore part of the electromagnetic model, not merely a cavity-resonance precaution.

Each core section receives its own effective conductivity, σ = L/(RA). The input section uses the entered input resistance, every interior section uses the stage resistance, and the output section uses the entered output resistance.

The MEEP branch includes only rotationally symmetric m = 0 fields. Leads, a helical MELF film, and asymmetric antenna or cavity modes require a later 3D solve.

What the repeating-cell approximation solves

For the all-bias field map, FEniCSx solves two unique axial regions:

  1. End region: the physical end cap through the axial center of the first bias plate.
  2. Interior half-cell: the center of that first bias plate through the center of the next interior ground plate—the second ground plate when the end cap is counted.

The far end is an exact mirror of the solved end region. Between the two ends, the interior half-cell is copied and alternately mirrored about successive bias-plate and ground-plate center planes until the reconstructed stack reaches the far-end region.

The two-region FEA provides the equal-voltage field map. A separate node-basis FEA extracts Cg and Cpar from Maxwell coefficients, reusing reflected and repeated excitation classes.

This reduction is never applied to asymmetric single-node basis fields. When unchecked, those capacitance fields use the full stack; only an exactly reflected partner excitation may be reused. The validation CLI can also disable reflection averaging for an independent full-model reference.

Calculation method

The temperature control evaluates thermally induced stress at a selected assembly temperature; it is not a design target.

This is a closed-form thermal-mismatch estimate, not a thermal-stress FEA. The estimate uses σ = kEεmismatch/(1 − ν). For DP270 and 832C, mismatch strain integrates the below- and above-Tg CTE over cooldown from the selected cure temperature. Bulk tensile comparisons use 45 MPa for 832C; DP270 and EP30 have no assigned bulk tensile limit. Existing Stycast screens retain their provisional 20 MPa proxy.

k is the fraction of radial and circumferential (hoop) mismatch strain prevented in the closed-form approximation. It assumes σrr = σθθ and σzz = σrz = 0. It is not a prescribed surface shift, and it does not control the FEA.

Axisymmetry removes variation with circumferential angle, but radial displacement still changes circumference, so hoop strain and stress remain. The FEA includes σrr, σzz, σθθ, and σrz. Local three-direction confinement can therefore exceed the radial-plus-hoop plane-stress baseline. At k = 0.5, the selected bulk-strength comparison crossing moves to --.

The FEniCSx model solves the selected plate and tube conductors, core, epoxy, and any distinct solid washer with axisymmetric linear elasticity and exact center-plane symmetry. Selecting an epoxy-filled gap removes the washer material interface. All remaining interfaces are perfectly bonded, so the model reports epoxy stress but cannot predict separation. A separation-temperature solve requires cohesive interface laws and adhesion or fracture data for the actual material pairs and surface preparations.

Cure shrinkage is not added as elastic strain because the locked-in fraction after gel is unknown. Any retained cure stress reduces the available cold margin.

Detector bias filtering

Discoidal Capacitor Bias Filter

A compact, axisymmetric high-voltage, low-current filter with a resistive core.

Simulation geometry: built-in defaults.

What Is Modeled

  • Electrostatics: axisymmetric FEniCSx fields, dielectric stress, and a node-resolved Maxwell capacitance matrix.
  • Circuit response: the complete unbuffered resistance-capacitance ladder, cable, detector, and DC load in SPICE.
  • Mechanical screening: a separate axisymmetric thermoelastic model for bonded epoxy, ceramic, copper, and core solids.
  • Symmetry and repeated cells are used only for excitations and regions that actually preserve them.

Electrical Model for SPICE Simulation

  • Rstage connects bias nodes and remains entered directly or calculated from resistive-core geometry.
  • Exact mode extracts each Cg,i and Cpar,i,j from full-stack Maxwell basis fields.
  • Fast repeated-cell mode uses a regional Cg allocation and analytical adjacent Cpar for screening.
  • End resistances occur once. Cable, detector, and load conductance appear only at the final node.
  • SPICE uses the selected exact or screening network without treating the two as equivalent evidence.

SPICE Simulation of the Complete Filter

  • The backend solves every R, Cg, and Cpar element in the unbuffered ladder.
  • Filter unloaded measures the bare output; the 50 Ω curve models matched test equipment at both ports.
  • HV regulator + input T-line adds the Designer regulator output resistance and source cable, while leaving the filter output unloaded.
  • Filter + output T-line + detector/LMFE drives the filter input and measures the reduced front-end response beyond the cryostat cable.
  • Everything starts at the regulator's ideal internal source and includes both transmission lines, the complete filter, detector, and amplifier.
  • The detector-facing curves apply the selected trapezoidal response and divide by the known-charge signal gain, giving detector-equivalent attenuation.
  • End resistors occur only where specified; no curve is made by multiplying an isolated one-section response.

Detector Front-End Pickup Model

  • The reduced ngspice model retains the MX11RC input JFET, BF862 cascode, 0.35 pF / 1 GΩ feedback, signal-cable delays, and the differential output across R30.
  • C19 uses the Designer/Electrical Detector C value; the 10 pF default is a conservative upper-end detector capacitance.
  • A 1 A AC test current is injected at the detector cathode to calculate pickup transimpedance through the selected cryostat HV cable.
  • The cable is terminated by the complete filter model, including its selected output resistance and capacitance network.
  • The LMH6654 stages are replaced by finite-gain, 10 MHz gain-bandwidth ideal op amps. Their bias filters and the later AD8139 differential-output stage are omitted.
  • The dedicated pickup slide reports transimpedance in V/A; the complete-filter slide uses the detector signal calibration to express that path as detector-equivalent attenuation.

Three Noise Sources in the Complete SPICE Model

  • Supply: the CAEN A1560 sketch shows a final series resistor, 1 nF to ground, and a 300 MΩ divider. Use 40 kΩ for the sketch's unspecified “few tens of k”, as selected for this study.
  • A1560H maximum ripple: 10 mV p-p at 20–1000 Hz; 5 mV p-p at 1 kHz–20 MHz. These terminal-ripple limits are applied conservatively upstream of the final RC for comparison; the internal HV filter and regulation loop are unspecified. Each band uses its worst-frequency sine scenario; the Designer power-line frequency is shown separately.
  • Outside cryostat: insert a series pickup voltage at the RG-58 filter end, retaining the regulator and entire input cable.
  • Inside cryostat: insert a separate series pickup voltage between the output cable and detector cathode, retaining the filter termination.
  • One source is active per AC solve. All three share the detector/LMFE and differential R30 output; noise powers are weighted by the trapezoid and calibrated with a known charge.
  • At low frequency the input cable contributes C = L/(Z0vfc), forming a T network between regulator R and filter input R. The transmission line already includes this capacitance.
  • Pickup voltages represent induced cable EMFs, not clamped node voltages. Cable-end placement approximates distributed pickup. The separate current-injection sensitivity studies use a different source convention.

Input-referred amplifier check

Simplified Amplifier SPICE Model

  • The reduced model preserves the detector capacitance, MX11RC J2 input device, BF862 cascode, 0.35 pF / 1 GΩ charge feedback, finite-gain amplifier stages, signal-cable delays, and differential R30 output.
  • The pickup solve excites the detector cathode. A separate 1 V AC solve excites the J2 gate and gives the amplifier signal gain Ggate(f).
  • Dividing pickup at R30 by |Ggate(f)| expresses each AC pickup component as an equivalent voltage at the J2 gate.
  • The trapezoidal response is identical in the numerator and denominator of this same-frequency ratio, so it cancels and is not needed for the input-referred check.
  • Energy-resolution broadening is calculated separately: shape the pickup spectrum, normalize it with the shaped response to a known detector-charge step, convert to equivalent energy, and combine it in quadrature with Fano noise.

Transient charge calibration

Detector Charge Sets the Energy Calibration

  • ngspice injects a known charge as a 100 ns current pulse from the detector cathode to the MX11RC J2 gate and measures the differential voltage across R30.
  • The collected charge is integrated onto the 0.35 pF feedback capacitor, producing the preamplifier step whose amplitude measures event energy.
  • The selected trapezoidal estimator is applied to this transient. Its shaped amplitude per unit charge is the scalar energy calibration.
  • Trap-filtered pickup RMS at R30 is divided by that amplitude and converted to equivalent charge, then to energy using 2.96 eV per electron-hole pair.
  • High-frequency rise-time and reflection distortion remain important for event-shape discrimination, but are evaluated separately from energy resolution.

Cryostat-cable pickup excitation

HV-Cable Pickup Transimpedance

  • ngspice injects 1 A AC at the C19 cathode node and calculates VR30/Ipickup. Its unit is V/A = Ω; it is not a dimensionless attenuation.
  • The complete filter terminates the far end of the 10 m cable with the selected output resistance.
  • The final-node Cg is connected from the last bias plate to ground, before the output resistor. It is not modeled as V16 source capacitance.
  • The output-stage Cg is swept from 0.1× through 100× its FEA-derived reference value.
  • The entered cryostat pickup voltage is represented as a Norton source Ipickup = Vpickup/Z0.
  • This is a reviewable lumped pickup convention; actual distributed coupling and common mode require cable measurements.

Pickup after energy filtering

Output Capacitance versus Energy Noise

  • Pickup voltage is represented by Ipickup = Vpickup/Z0, propagated through the cable and LMFE, and measured across R30.
  • The Designer/Noise cryostat power-line and broadband RMS values set the pickup amplitude.
  • The selected discrete trapezoidal response is applied before line and broadband terms are combined in quadrature.
  • The preceding known-charge transient converts the shaped R30 RMS voltage into equivalent detector-energy RMS.
  • The seven solved capacitances span 0.1×, 0.3×, 1×, 3×, 10×, 30×, and 100× the reference output-stage Cg.

Termination sensitivity at three capacitances

Output Resistance versus Energy Noise

  • The same LMFE pickup calculation is repeated while varying the resistance between the final bias plate and the 10 m cryostat HV cable.
  • Each resistance is solved with 0.1×, 1×, and 10× the FEA-derived output-stage Cg.
  • The selected trapezoidal filter is applied to pickup at R30, then normalized by the shaped known-charge transient; no frequency-by-frequency gate-gain division is used.
  • The sweep includes the ordered 10, 22, 27, 33, 39, 47, and 51 Ω MELF values plus nearby points that reveal the trend.
  • At the design capacitance, 22-51 Ω produces practically the same equivalent energy noise; termination is therefore selected for fast-step fidelity rather than energy resolution.
  • The cable model gives Z0 ≈ 48 Ω and about 47.5 ns one-way delay. With a sub-100 ns signal rise, transient reflections and pulse-shape fidelity are more relevant than a 100 kHz spot value.

Noise-based stopping point

Bias and Cable Noise After the Energy Filter

  • A 2039 keV event creates about 689,000 electron-hole pairs, or 110 fC. The three-measurement weighted default F = 0.1145 gives about 831 eV σ of irreducible Fano fluctuation.
  • CAEN supply ripple passes through the 40 kΩ / 1 nF CAEN output network, RG-58 input cable, filter, cryostat cable, detector, amplifier, and trapezoidal energy estimator.
  • Outside and inside cryostat pickup are separate localized series voltage excitations, with all passive components retained. Entered pickup voltages describe induced EMFs, not clamped node voltages.
  • Each R30 noise response uses the shaped known-charge signal calibration. If complete SPICE results are missing, the graphic lists the simulations to run.
  • With the sinusoidal conversion used in this model, the A1560H ripple limits are 3.54 mV σ at 20–1000 Hz and 1.77 mV σ at 1 kHz–20 MHz. Band powers are added; the low-band scenario at the Designer power-line frequency is an alternative, never counted twice.
  • The public LEGEND reference estimator uses a 10 µs rise and 4 µs flat top; current production settings remain an input to confirm.
  • The design criterion is predicted bias-path noise below 0.10 times the calculated Fano σ; observed detector resolution is deliberately excluded.
  • Both pickup sources default to 0.20 mV σ at the selected power-line frequency (50 Hz) plus 0.05 mV σ broadband. Independent powers are added; a separate same-phase mains sum shows possible coherent interference.

Bias-to-Ground Capacitance from FEA

  • A one-bias geometry gives Cg directly because no bias-to-bias capacitance exists.
  • In the three-bias geometry, the middle-only result Cm includes capacitance to both grounded neighboring bias plates and therefore overcounts Cg.
  • The neighbors-only and all-bias states remove that mutual contribution: Cg,m = (Cm − Cn + Ca)/2.
  • Exact full-design values use the Maxwell row sum Cg,i = ΣjCij; repeated screening values do not claim this extraction.
  • The resistive core remains dielectric during capacitance extraction and is not treated as an electrode.

Bias-to-Bias Parasitic Capacitance from FEA

  • The panels show the actual full-stack r-z region and representative boundary potentials returned by FEniCSx.
  • The brown core is dielectric with its selected effective permittivity; only the solid bias-plate cross sections are electrodes.
  • Exact reflected basis fields are reused. For two bias plates, node B is the mirror of node A and is not solved separately.
  • The mirrored field is reused directly in the Maxwell cross-energy integral; the all-bias energy remains an independent total check.
  • For three plates, only one end, the center, and all-bias states are solved; the far-end basis is reused by reflection.
  • The two-node panel is reconstructed from the linear basis fields as a visual check, not an additional solve.
  • Cpar,i,j is the negative off-diagonal Maxwell coefficient, −Cij.

Peak Electric Field from FEA

Axisymmetric thermoelastic solution

Thermal FEA Model

  • FEniCSx solves the copper plates and tube, resistive core, and continuous DP270 dielectric as bonded elastic solids in cylindrical r-z symmetry.
  • Axial center-plane mirror symmetry and cylindrical symmetry are exact for the idealized geometry.
  • The DP270 default uses a provisional 40 °C stress-free reference and cooldown to 20 °C. Cure duration at 40 °C is unverified; E = 3 GPa is assumed. CTE integrates across the 43 °C Tg onset for hotter schedules.
  • The MELF body is modeled as its dominant Al2O3 ceramic substrate; the thin resistive film, steel caps, plating, and coating are omitted.
  • External surfaces are traction-free; every material interface is perfectly bonded.
  • The model includes radial, axial, hoop, and r-z shear stress, but excludes cure shrinkage, viscoelastic relaxation, voids, cohesive debonding, and fracture.
  • The color field is epoxy maximum-principal tensile stress. P99 is used for comparison; the marked raw corner peak remains mesh sensitive.

Frequency-domain model ladder

From Damped Resonances to Field-Derived Transfer

  • The frequency-domain network combines the FEniCSx Maxwell capacitance matrix with geometry-derived partial inductance for each axial current path.
  • The 350 mΩ low-loss reference exposes the coupled LC natural modes; it is not the physical filter resistance.
  • A single-mode RLC check chooses Leff so its zero-resistance natural frequency matches the first coupled-model peak, then applies the same physical resistance.
  • For an underdamped mode, the physical-resistance peak follows fpeak = f0√(1 − 2ζ2). With the default 10 MΩ stages the model is overdamped, so it predicts no shifted amplitude peak.

Earlier prototype trade

Ferrite path: set aside

  • Multimeter resistance readings were too noisy and unstable to support a predictable first prototype.

MELF path: retained

  • Nearly the same diameter permits comparison in the same mechanical envelope.

Continuous Epoxy Removes the Washer Seam

Using the potting compound as the complete plate-to-ground dielectric removes the separate washer/potting boundary. In the washer construction, that boundary can lie approximately parallel to the axial electric field.

One homogeneous dielectric region removes that seam. Metal/epoxy and core/epoxy bonds, bulk voids, cure shrinkage, and thermal cracking still require process control and qualification.

Backup: optional symmetric shunt

Avoid High-K Capacitors Without PD Qualification

Why nominal C is not enough

  • High-permittivity ferroelectric ceramics can lose substantial effective capacitance under DC bias.
  • Thin dielectric layers, internal electrode edges, pores, cracks, and termination geometry create local fields that a voltage rating alone does not describe.
  • Published BaTiO3 studies show that porosity and void size strongly affect partial-discharge inception.

Keep it a qualified fallback

  • Use geometry-derived Cg as the baseline and add discrete capacitance only if the attenuation requirement demands it.
  • If needed, use matched parts symmetrically and qualify the complete potted assembly at operating DC plus realistic ripple and transients.
  • Measure PD, leakage, effective biased capacitance, loss, microphonics, and thermal-cycle stability.

Design decision: do not use high-k ceramic capacitors in the reference build without part-level and assembly-level DC partial-discharge evidence. The circuit input remains available for sensitivity studies.

Backup: prototype test sequence

Why Ferrite Was Set Aside

  1. Original attraction. Type 61 material could provide much more resistance per unit length than available MELF parts.
  2. Observed problem. Resistance measurements with a multimeter were noisy and insufficiently stable for a predictable bias-filter element.
  3. Noise concern. A polycrystalline conduction path also lacks the established excess-noise behavior of a metal-film resistor.
  4. Current decision. Do not build the present prototype around ferrite; retain the model and sourcing notes only as an engineering record.
  5. Condition for revisiting. Ferrite would require repeatable guarded resistance measurements and voltage-noise spectra under representative DC bias.

Status: ferrite and ferrite/MELF hybrid concepts are no longer under consideration unless later measurements overturn the resistance-stability and noise concerns.

Backup: circuit model

One Section Matches; Stage Multiplication Does Not

  • For one Rstage–Cg section with no additional end resistance or external load, the analytical divider and full MNA/SPICE solve are identical.
  • With multiple unbuffered sections, downstream capacitors load the upstream nodes, so their transfer functions cannot be multiplied as independent stages.
  • The section-count approximation also repeats the cable and detector load at every section instead of applying it once at the output.
  • The full ladder solve, not the multiplied estimate, is used for design conclusions.

Backup: Cpar solver consistency

Full, Mirror, and Repeated Models Agree

  • The reference meshes the complete stack and applies no symmetry to the extracted matrix.
  • The mirror case averages only exactly reflected Maxwell-matrix entries.
  • The repeated case additionally shares fully interior entries; end coupling remains distinct.
  • For two through five bias plates, all three methods give the same average adjacent Cpar.
  • The default geometry gives FEA/analytic ≈ 0.478 because the intervening ground plate shields the axial path.

Backup: Cpar analytical limit

Cpar Converges Toward the Axial Approximation

  • Use three bias plates in a uniform εr = 3.4 dielectric.
  • Make the ground plate a narrow outer annulus and move it and the grounded tube radially outward.
  • Increase the bias-disc diameter with the ground-hole diameter so most field energy is axial.
  • FEA/εA/d rises monotonically from 0.720 at 24 mm OD to 0.949 at 120 mm OD.
  • This limiting geometry verifies that ordinary designs fall below the parallel-plate estimate because of shielding, not a capacitance scaling error.

Backup: Cg end effects

Cg Matrix: Two vs Three Bias Plates

  • The two-bias matrix has one unique end-node basis field; the opposite end is its exact reflection.
  • The three-bias matrix has two unique basis fields: one end and the center. The far end reuses the first field by reflection.
  • Comparing the two-bias value with the three-bias end and center values exposes finite-stack end effects.
  • The reflected field supplies the remaining cross-energy coefficients; the all-bias energy independently verifies the row-sum total.
  • For two or three plates, Repeat interior adds no further class: there is either no interior node or only one.

Backup: Cg solver consistency

Cg Matrix Treatments Agree

  • The campaign includes the previously omitted one-bias case, then two through five bias plates.
  • The reference uses the complete stack with no matrix symmetry; exact mirror averages only reflected entries.
  • Repeating treatment additionally shares interior-node classes while preserving total Cg.

Backup: Cg analytical limit

Cg Converges Toward Two Flat Washers

  • Use one bias plate between two ground plates so center-plane mirror symmetry is exact.
  • Use a 2.0 mm core, 2.2 mm ground ID, and uniform εr = 3.4 dielectric.
  • Increase matched washer and bias OD from 12 to 120 mm while retaining a 2 mm radial tube gap.
  • FEA/(2εA/d) falls monotonically from 1.449 to 1.038.
  • The positive excess is fringing capacitance; its fractional contribution shrinks as plate area grows.

Backup: Cg validation audit trail

The Limiting-Case Harness Needed Correction

  • Initial three-bias wide-disc batches were stopped near 5.7 GiB RSS before swap or OOM.
  • The primary cause was a 0.01 mm radial gap imposing a 0.024 mm global coarse target over the large disc.
  • An interim retained-memory diagnosis was incomplete; the completed mesh records exposed the global overrefinement.
  • The first isolated subprocess launch also had a sys-shadowing bug and failed before FEA.
  • The final test uses one bias plate, exact mirror symmetry, a 0.10 mm radial gap, and isolated diameter cases; swap remained zero.

Backup: limiting geometry

Radius and Gap Set a Conservative Field Bound

  • Let r be the minimum radius on any exposed conductor feature.
  • Let d be the minimum dielectric gap to the opposite conductor.
  • The limiting reference is a radius-r sphere inside a radius-(r + d) grounded sphere.
  • Use Emax = V(r + d)/(r d), then audit the actual geometry with FEA.

Backup: geometry optimization

Field-Limited Packing Has a Simple Shape Optimum

  • Assume plate thickness is 2r, dielectric gap is d, and stack pitch is L = d + 2r.
  • Combine gap capacitance with the radius-gap field limit.
  • The resulting capacitance-per-length-per-field metric is optimized at r/d = 1/√2.
